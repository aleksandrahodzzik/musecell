This was a true positive for Microtubule stabilizers and the evidence was unusually direct. Unblinding shows epothilone B, PubChem CID 448013, ChEMBL CHEMBL94657, annotated as Tubulin stabiliser - target CHEMBL2095182 - name and BBBC021 structure agree, which is consistent with the known literature that epothilone B is a microtubule stabilizer PMID 12802795 and PMID 39147581.

What we got right and why:

1. Acquisition choice: Starting with highest dose w_56c0ae0535 3 uM (e2) immediately revealed maximal phenotype: tub_glcm_contrast z=+121.7, tub_std z=+69.7, nuc_solidity z=-39.8 with 137 cells. Adding w_82fe9cfd0d 1 uM (e5) tub_glcm_contrast +117.6, tub_std +87.2, tub_mean +58.7, then w_b414b296e0 0.1 uM (e3) tub_std +33.1, tub_glcm_contrast +28.0, and w_a15719a464 0.01 uM (e4) tub_glcm_contrast +5.5, tub_std +5.2 gave a graded curve. That 4-well sampling across 0.01 to 3 uM of the available 8 doses (e1) was sufficient to see threshold at 0.01 uM and saturation by 1-3 uM. We left 1 well unused but did not need it.

2. Measurement interpretation: The extreme tubulin texture/contrast gain rather than loss correctly discriminated stabilizer vs destabilizer. Microtubule destabilizers would decrease tubulin polymer mass and mean intensity; here tub_mean rho=+0.80 (e8), tub_std and tub_glcm_contrast increase with dose, and describe_phenotype (e6) independently noted density lower vs DMSO with 6 observations favoring Microtubule stabilizers - consistent with bundling and mitotic arrest, not microtubule loss. act_tub_ratio rho=-0.80 (e11) reflects rising tubulin relative to actin, contradicting Actin disruptors.

3. Comparison: compare_to_reference_atlas (e7) 5/10 nearest neighbors Microtubule stabilizers, 2/10 Protein degradation, 1/10 Cholesterol-lowering. As the review noted, the stabilizer neighbors dominated and the protein degradation hits were lower-ranked; we correctly downweighted them.

Nuclear phenotype dose response: nuc_solidity rho=-1.00 (e9) and frac_large_nucleus rho=+0.80 (e12) with cell_count rho=-0.80 (e10) captured dose-dependent lobulation/polyploidy and arrest/cytotoxicity. We correctly did not attribute this in isolation to Aurora kinase inhibitors - which can share polyploidy - because Aurora inhibitors do not predict the +60 to +120z tubulin bundling/gain seen in e2/e5.

What could have misled and what we missed:

- Aurora kinase/Eg5 inhibitors were the main alternative to exclude, because they also produce large nuclei and solidity loss. Without the tubulin channel, e9/e12 alone would have been ambiguous. The discrimination rested entirely on e2/e3/e5 tubulin gain/bundling and e6 microscopy. If we had only acquired low dose e4 (+5z), we would have under-called.
- Protein degradation 2/10 neighbors in e7 could have been distracting, but the magnitude difference (+120z vs typical proteasome phenotypes) made it implausible. We did not acquire additional markers to formally rule it out.
- Dose_response statistics were underpowered: N=4 wells, p=0.2 for e8, e10, e11, e12, p=0 for e9. We correctly relied on effect size and monotonicity rather than p-value threshold, but we did not add a replicate well at same high dose to estimate technical variance, nor did we image tubulin post-translational modifications. The stated next experiment - acetylated/detyrosinated tubulin immunofluorescence with phospho-H3 and a polymerization assay vs destabilizer control - is appropriate.
- Literature search 1 returned 116 hits for epothilone B microtubule stabilizer (including PMID 12802795, PMID 39147581, PMID 18593821, PMID 25765066, PMID 41224993) and search 2 correctly returned 0 hits for generic bundling/arrest phrase, so no contradictory literature burdened the call.

Overall hypothesis updating to 0.93 stabilizers vs 0.02 destabilizer/Aurora at step 13 (e13) and submission (e14) was warranted and approved; unblinding confirms no correction needed.
